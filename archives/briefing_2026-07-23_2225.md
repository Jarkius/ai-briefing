## 🎯 Prompt Engineering Tips

**Ask for evidence before accepting bold AI claims**  
When a vendor or article makes a big claim, prompt your AI assistant to separate facts, claims, assumptions, and missing evidence. Example: “Review this announcement. Create a table with verified facts, company claims, unanswered questions, and tests we should run before buying.” This works well for chip, model, and infrastructure announcements where hype can hide practical risks.  
**Key takeaway:** Use this when evaluating AI vendors, hardware, or new tools before making a business decision.  
📱 Social post: Prompt tip: ask AI to split “facts, claims, assumptions, and missing evidence” before you trust a bold tech announcement. #PromptEngineering #AITips #AIBusiness  
[Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/23/ai-chip-startup-etched-defies-skeptics-hits-10-3b-valuation-from-big-name-investors/)

**Use a “test plan” prompt for model optimization**  
For technical changes like 4-bit diffusion inference, ask the AI to design a comparison plan instead of simply explaining the feature. Example: “Create a test plan comparing standard diffusion inference with 4-bit inference. Include speed, memory, quality, failure cases, and acceptance criteria.” This turns a tutorial into an actionable evaluation workflow.  
**Key takeaway:** Use this when deciding whether a faster or cheaper AI method is good enough for production.  
📱 Social post: Don’t just ask “what is 4-bit inference?” Ask AI for a test plan: speed, memory, quality, edge cases, and acceptance criteria. #PromptEngineering #AITips #GenAI  
[Source](https://huggingface.co/blog/nunchaku-diffusers)

**Turn security disclosures into an action checklist**  
Security posts can be dense, so prompt AI to convert them into role-based tasks. Example: “Summarize this security disclosure for executives, developers, and IT admins. Create a checklist of actions, owners, urgency, and open questions.” Always verify the summary against the original disclosure before acting, especially for incident details.  
**Key takeaway:** Use this to move from awareness to action after vendor security updates.  
📱 Social post: Prompt tip: paste a vendor security disclosure and ask for a role-based checklist with owners, urgency, and open questions. Then verify against the source. #PromptEngineering #AISecurity #AITips  
[Source](https://huggingface.co/blog/security-incident-july-2026)

**Prompt for vendor risk, not just product features**  
When reading reports about AI company debt or financial pressure, ask your AI assistant to assess continuity risk. Example: “Based on this article, list possible vendor risks, what is confirmed vs alleged, questions to ask procurement, and contract protections to consider.” Mark claims as reported or alleged unless they are verified by primary documents.  
**Key takeaway:** Use this before signing contracts with AI providers whose economics may affect service stability or pricing.  
📱 Social post: Prompt beyond features. Ask AI to assess vendor continuity risk: confirmed facts, alleged claims, procurement questions, and contract protections. #PromptEngineering #AITips #RiskManagement  
[Source](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-companies-hide-debt-off-balance-sheet)

**Define the data dictionary inside your prompt**  
For location or policy tasks, include the exact definitions your model should use. Example: “Use OpenStreetMap admin_level definitions. Before answering, identify the country and relevant administrative level, then state any ambiguity.” This reduces errors caused by terms that change meaning across countries or datasets.  
**Key takeaway:** Use this whenever prompts rely on specialized categories, labels, or regional definitions.  
📱 Social post: Better prompt: include the data dictionary. Tell AI which definitions to use, especially for geography, policy, finance, or legal categories. #PromptEngineering #AITips #DataLiteracy

Focus on maintainability, security, hiring, deployment risk, and migration cost.” This helps non-specialists understand why engineering foundations affect AI delivery.

**AI agents need permission limits**  
Allen Institute for AI’s Shippy write-up discusses lessons from building agents, which is directly relevant to security. AI agents can take multi-step actions, call tools, retrieve data, and make changes faster than a human reviewer can track. Without guardrails, an agent can expose sensitive data, perform unintended actions, or make errors that look authoritative.
